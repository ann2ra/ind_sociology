## 3. What I did with the output

Not a single accept/reject: the work was iterative, and I made the key methodological decisions myself.

- **Fixed the data import with the AI, but chose the route.** The AI first pointed to the `.dta` file; I corrected it that I only had a Stata `.do` script and a `.sav` file (prompt 3), so we switched to `foreign::read.spss`, which worked.
- **Chose the indicators myself.** I picked belief in God (F050) for believing, and both attendance and prayer for practice. I wanted a "once a year" threshold; after the AI explained that in France yearly attendance mostly captures Christmas, weddings and funerals, I kept **both** thresholds (monthly and yearly) and explain the difference in the scenario instead of choosing only one.
- **Caught that the AI's first code had a wrong threshold.** After running the diagnostics, the AI itself corrected "at least once a year" from F028 ≤ 5 to F028 ≤ 6 once the real 8-category coding was visible. I re-ran the tables with the corrected code.
- **Chose the forecast method against the AI's first recommendation.** The AI recommended a simple "generations keep their level" model; I chose to add within-generation **drift** (prompt 9) and asked for UN projections instead of the AI's suggested INSEE data when the INSEE site failed (prompt 14).
- **Questioned the method when the self-test looked weak** (prompts 23–25). The back-cast showed the "nothing changes" (naive) forecast beat our model for 4 of 6 indicators. I proposed using the naive forecast as the centre; after the AI explained the difference between the naive and the no-drift model, I chose a compromise (variant B): drift only for belonging (where the test showed it clearly helps), no drift for the other indicators, and the naive forecast as a possible upper bound.
- **Widened the range after the back-cast showed two misses** (prompt 28), using survey sampling error (with an effective sample size because of the weights) rather than an ad hoc upward shift, because it is statistically justified. I asked for a worked example on our own numbers before accepting it (prompts 29–30).
- **Scenario text:** I wrote the structure, and the AI drafted the text. I then asked for a much shorter, popular-science methodology section (prompt 34), corrected the wording so every number has a year (prompt 35), and chose two extra story sections to make the text less dry (prompt 36). I compared the model results with my own `MY_FORECAST.md`: my guess about believing and practice was right in direction, my guess that belonging would stay stable was wrong.

**Claim:** the AI stated that in variable F025 ("Religious denomination, major groups") of the EVS/WVS joint file, **code 5 = Muslim**, and on that basis calculated the Muslim share among French respondents (0.4% in 1981 → 5.8% in 2018).

**Source checked against:** the codebook of the joint EVS/WVS dataset (GESIS, ZA7503), value labels for F025. The labels did not show up in R (`attr(fr$F025, "value.labels")` returned NULL), so I checked the codebook directly.

**Result:** confirmed, code 5 = Muslim. The Muslim share in the survey (5.8% in 2018) is lower than the ≈10% of 18–59-year-olds reported by INSEE/INED (Trajectoires et Origines 2, 2019–2020), so I added to the scenario's limitations that the survey probably under-represents Muslims.
